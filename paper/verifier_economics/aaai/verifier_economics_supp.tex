\section{Soundness and the Strictness Nesting}\label{supp:proof}
\begin{theorem}[Strictness nesting]\label{thm:nest}
For a target structure $T$ and the RNA rungs of \S\ref{supp:rnasat}, for any Boltzmann threshold
$\theta>\tfrac12$ the accept-sets satisfy $\pfe\subseteq\umfe\subseteq\match$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
If $\PT\ge\theta>\tfrac12$ then $T$ has strictly greater equilibrium probability than any other structure
(their probabilities sum to $\le1-\theta<\theta$), so $T$ is the unique MFE structure: $\pfe\subseteq\umfe$.
If $T$ is the unique MFE structure then in particular $\mathrm{MFE}(s)=T$: $\umfe\subseteq\match$. Both
inclusions can be strict (a sequence can fold to $T$ as its MFE without $T$ dominating the ensemble). \end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sound cascade, $\epsilon{=}0$]\label{prop:cascade}
Let $\mathcal{A}_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_k$ be nested accept-sets ordered by increasing cost,
$\mathcal{A}_k$ the gold rung. The cheapest-first cascade---evaluate in order, reject at the first rejecting
rung, escalate to gold only if all cheaper rungs accept---returns $\mathbf{1}[x\in\mathcal{A}_k]$ on every
$x$, and evaluates gold only on $x\in\mathcal{A}_{k-1}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If some $j<k$ rejects $x$ then by nesting $\mathcal{A}_k\subseteq\mathcal{A}_j$, so $x\notin\mathcal{A}_k$ and
the early reject equals the gold reject. Otherwise $x$ reaches and is decided by gold. The gold rung fires
only when all cheaper rungs accept, i.e.\ $x\in\mathcal{A}_{k-1}$. \end{proof}
On a stream with penultimate-rung accept-rate $\alpha$, expected cost over always-gold is
$\alpha + (1-\alpha)\,c_{\mathrm{cheap}}/c_{\mathrm{gold}}$, so the saving $(1-\alpha)(1-c_{\mathrm{cheap}}/
c_{\mathrm{gold}})$ is largest on reject-heavy streams (small $\alpha$)---e.g.\ early RL training.

\section{RNA/SAT Verifier Definitions, Oracle Validity, Native Control}\label{supp:rnasat}
\paragraph{RNA ladder.} For target $T$ and designed sequence $s$ under a fixed folding model
\citep{lorenz2011viennarna}: $\match$ accepts iff $\mathrm{MFE}(s)=T$; $\umfe$ iff $T$ is the unique MFE
structure; $\pfe$ iff the target Boltzmann probability $\PT\ge\theta$ (an ensemble-defect--style objective
\citep{zadeh2011nupack}). The pseudoknot branch replaces the MFE oracle with a pseudoknot-aware predictor
(pKiss \citep{janssen2015pkiss}); the MFE ladder cannot represent crossing pairs, so for a pseudoknot target
it is out-of-domain.
\paragraph{Oracle validity of the $28\%$ (Axis 1).} The conditional false-accept rate for state-of-the-art
pseudoknot-aware designs \citep{wirecki2025desirna} is itself an oracle verdict, so we report its dependence
rather than a point estimate. Under the pKiss reference it is $28\%$; the fixed-single-design cross-oracle
envelope across three independent predictors spans $30$--$88\%$ (vs.\ $15$--$60\%$ best-of-pool). The
qualitative conclusion is invariant: under \emph{no} pseudoknot-aware reference does the declared crossing
stem form in more than a small minority of skeleton-``solved'' designs (it forms in $0/250$ of the
blind-search designs). \paragraph{Native positive control.} As a control that the pseudoknot predictors are
not simply pessimistic, we fold \emph{native} pseudoknot sequences (which are known to form the crossing
pairs) under the same three-predictor panel; they recover the crossing stems at the expected high rate,
establishing that the near-zero recovery on skeleton designs is a property of those designs, not of the
oracle panel. \paragraph{SAT mirror.} Satisfying-assignment check $\supseteq$ unit-propagation-robust
$\supseteq$ Hamming-ball-robust; SAT candidates come from a WalkSAT-style local search
\citep{selman1994walksat}. The Axis-2 hardness trend is confirmed \emph{selection-free} by exhaustive
enumeration \citep{ardelius2008exhaustive}, tracking the random-SAT clustering/freezing transition
\citep{krzakala2007clustering,zhou2008frozen}, so it is a property of cheap-proxy verification, not of RNA or
of a generator's selection.

\section{Controlled Transfer-Failure (Goodhart) Study}\label{supp:goodhart}
With the proposer fixed and only the reward varied (RL-free), we optimize either the cheap exact-match proxy
(the reward used by RL RNA designers \citep{runge2019learna}) or a robustness-aligned reward, at matched
optimization budgets. The cheap arm drives its own proxy $31\%\!\to\!70\%$ while robustness stays flat
($18\%\!\to\!16\%$); the aligned arm climbs robustness $12\%\!\to\!33\%$. The hierarchical
arm$\times$budget difference-in-differences is $+23.3$ points with a target-label permutation test
$p{=}0.0002$, and it survives a matched-\emph{wall-time} control ($+18.9$ at equal compute), so it is not an
artifact of the cheap arm running more steps. This is over-optimization of the proxy
\citep{gao2023overoptimization} localized to the verifier; a policy-gradient replication reproduces the
\emph{direction} on easy targets without a dramatic collapse, consistent with the effect being
reward-attributable yet optimizer-modulated. We flag the weaker within-cheap-arm peak-then-decline as
suggestive, not load-bearing.

\section{End-to-End Cost Model}\label{supp:cost}
Per-call cost is dominated by the oracle's algorithmic complexity, then by setup reuse. In RNA all rungs share
the $O(n^3)$ partition-function/folding machinery (measured empirical scaling $\sim n^{2.8}$ on
$\le150$\,nt targets) and reuse the fold-compound setup, so strictness is a small constant ($\sim\!4\times$
from $\match$ to $\pfe$). In SAT robustness needs a higher-complexity Hamming-ball neighborhood scan, so the
strong rung is $\sim\!400\times$ the assignment check. The two-order-of-magnitude contrast is the point: the
price of strictness is set by \emph{whether the strong rung shares the cheap oracle's complexity}, not by a
generic ``stronger is costlier.'' In the RLVR loop the math verifier is shared-complexity (parsing/evaluating a
boxed value vs.\ scanning for a number), so routing saves a small constant, consistent with the RNA case and
against the SAT case.

\section{LLM Code-Verifier Protocol (HumanEval+)}\label{supp:code}
On HumanEval+ \citep{evalplus} we run two open-weights proposers (a strong one, Qwen3.6-27B, and a weak one,
Llama-3.1-8B \citep{llama3_2024}), scoring on held-out gold tests under a fixed seed-$0$ split, with all
completions and sandbox diagnostics saved. The cheap rung is ``passes the provided base tests''; the strong
rung is ``passes a held-out gold test.'' The conditional false-accept rate (passes base, fails gold) is
$2.4\%$ (strong proposer) and $28.4\%$ (weak), an order of magnitude larger where it matters; strengthening
the rung monotonically cuts it in both. The Goodhart-\emph{under-selection} best-of-$12$ gap on held-out gold
is a null ($+0.0$/$+0.6$), which we report as such rather than claim a code-domain selection effect.

\section{RLVR Configuration and Per-Family Results}\label{supp:rlvr}
\paragraph{Policies/data.} Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct \citep{qwen25_2024} and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{llama3_2024}. Train prompts: NuminaMath-CoT \citep{numinamath2024} restricted to numeric-canonical
gold; held-out: $500$ MATH-source problems excluded from training. DAPO-style difficulty filtering
\citep{dapo2025} (base pass-rate $1$--$7$ of $8$) retains $7{,}113/24{,}000$ prompts for 7B ($32\%$
all-wrong, $38\%$ all-right groups discarded as zero-gradient).
\paragraph{GRPO.} $8$ generations/prompt, temperature $1.0$, $\beta{=}0.001$, $\epsilon{=}0.2/0.28$,
token-level importance sampling, full-parameter tuning, bf16, vLLM colocate rollouts; $2$ GPUs/run
(data-parallel), $2000$-step runs peak $\sim\!272/275$\,GB. Standard budget $800$ steps, lr
$2{\times}10^{-6}$; long-horizon probe $2000$ steps, lr $5{\times}10^{-6}$. All arms share every setting
except the reward verifier. Metrics per step: strong-verified true accuracy, $\J{=}\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR}$
against strong gold (locating the run relative to the $\J{=}0$ boundary \citep{rateorfate2026}), gaming rate
(cheap-accept $\wedge$ strong-reject), and cumulative strong-verifier calls. Reported runs: 3B pilot
($5$ runs), 7B main ($3$ arms $\times2$ seeds, $800$ steps), 7B long-horizon ($3$ arms $+$ a second strong
seed, $2000$ steps), Llama-3.1-8B ($3$ arms). The 3B pilot's flat held-out is diagnosed to the zero-variance
groups that difficulty filtering removes; the long-horizon budget is the regime in which the strong arm
itself moves held-out, the only regime in which a held-out quality claim is meaningful.

\section{The Risk-Calibrated Dial and Routing Calibration}\label{supp:dial}
Between \textsc{cheap} (escalate none) and \textsc{cascade} (escalate all cheap-accepts) sits a one-parameter
family: escalate a cheap-accept to strong with probability $p$, decided by a deterministic content hash
$u(x)=\mathrm{sha256}(\text{completion}\,\|\,\text{gold})/2^{64}\in[0,1)$, escalating iff $u(x)<p$. The hash
makes the decision reproducible and identical across data-parallel workers (unlike a per-process RNG). Expected
verifier cost is $c_{\mathrm{cheap}}+p\cdot(\text{cheap-accept rate})\cdot c_{\mathrm{strong}}$ (monotone
increasing in $p$); expected rewarded-gaming is $(1-p)\cdot(\text{gaming rate})$ (monotone decreasing).
$p{=}0$ recovers \textsc{cheap}, $p{=}1$ recovers \textsc{cascade} exactly; interior $p$ traces the continuous
cost$\times$robustness frontier (main Fig.~2). \paragraph{Stop-and-accept calibration.} A rung-based
stop-and-accept rule is admissible only if its false-positive rate has a Clopper--Pearson
\citep{clopperpearson1934} upper bound $\le\epsilon$. On a deliberately reject-heavy workload ($n{=}195$
candidates, $13$ per target) the cheap rung accepts $10$ candidates while gold accepts $0$: all $10$ are false
positives ($10/10$, one-sided $95\%$ upper bound $1.0$), so no cheaper-than-gold rung clears any $\epsilon<1$.
This is a demonstrated, workload-specific incompressibility, not a general theorem; a different proposer, gold
prevalence, or training stage gives a different rate. The mechanism that generalizes: once a cheap rung is
highly gameable---the regime optimization pressure produces---the strong rung must run.

\section{Reproduction}\label{supp:repro}
All verifier arms, the difficulty filter, the GRPO reward plugin, the held-out evaluator, and the
aggregation/figure scripts accompany the paper. Every reported RLVR number is emitted by a single generator
from raw run logs (no hand-transcription); the dial escalation is a pure function of content, so a rerun with
the same seed reproduces per-sample escalation decisions bit-for-bit. RNA uses ViennaRNA
\citep{lorenz2011viennarna} and pKiss \citep{janssen2015pkiss}; SAT uses a WalkSAT-style solver
\citep{selman1994walksat} with an exhaustive Hamming-ball checker \citep{ardelius2008exhaustive}; the code
study uses HumanEval+ \citep{evalplus} under a fixed seed-$0$ split.
