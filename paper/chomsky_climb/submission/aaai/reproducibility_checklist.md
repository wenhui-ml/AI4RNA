# Reproducibility checklist

Answers follow the AAAI checklist headings. Where an answer is "no" it says so and says why, because a
checklist whose every box is ticked carries no information.

## This paper

**Makes claims about a computational artifact.** Yes. It audits a design operator and reports six evidence
components plus three attribution rungs.

**Clearly states what claims are and are not made.** Yes, and this is the paper's organising principle.
Table 1 grades our own system component-wise (one exact, three established, one weak, one not established);
Table 2 grades the arm that clears the bar. Two things are explicitly not claimed: a robust autonomous ascent
by our operator, and any scientific discovery of a design principle by anyone here.

**Limitations discussed.** Yes, §6.2, in rough order of how much each bounds the contribution, including the
one that caps every positive number (the three panel predictors share thermodynamic parameters).

## Theoretical contributions

**Each claim is formally stated.** Yes: Lemma 1, Proposition 1, Definitions 1–4, Remarks 1–3.

**Proofs are provided.** Yes, App. A. Proposition 1's proof is one paragraph and the paper says so rather
than dressing it up. Results invoked from the literature (non-context-freeness of crossing arcs, the MCFG-2
characterisation) are cited, not re-proved, and App. A states exactly which of the two we verify ourselves
and why (the family we construct is not covered by either citation).

**Assumptions stated.** Yes, and one is load-bearing: $\Omega_{cf}$ is fixed as a *named function*
(ViennaRNA's `RNA.fold`), not as a software package. The same distribution ships `RNAPKplex`, which does emit
crossings; §2 states that it is never called and that the exclusion would not apply to a system whose verifier
were `RNAPKplex`.

## Datasets

**All datasets are publicly available or included.** Yes. Pseudobase++ via RNAInvBench; the cross-source pool
from bpRNA/ArchiveII via RNAInvBench; EteRNA100-V2. The synthetic family $\mathcal{L}_H$ is generated by
`family_grid.py` in the supplement.

**Preprocessing described.** Yes, App. D, including the multi-bracket reconstruction of crossing dot-bracket
from the cross-source pair lists and the exclusion of multiplet structures.

**Train/test split described.** Yes, and the honest answer is unflattering: the primary $n{=}60$ pool is a
**development/evaluation** pool, not a held-out benchmark, and the paper calls it that throughout. The
ID-disjoint pool is disjoint by target identity but not by structure ($39/125$ exact structural duplicates).
The structurally clean split has only 27 targets in 24 clusters, which is a property of the benchmark.

## Experiments

**Code is included.** Yes, `code_and_data.zip`.

**All results are reproducible from the included code and data.** Yes for every measurement, with one
scoped exception. Measurements re-execute frozen source over committed inputs. The **invention step** is not
fully reproducible: the six operators were written by frontier models behind a routed provider alias that
exposes no version hash, so a re-run months later may not query the same weights. No number in the paper
depends on it, because every number re-executes the frozen operator source rather than re-querying a model,
but the claim "an agent can invent such an operator" does. §6.2(v) states this.

**Hyperparameters specified.** Yes, App. F, with the oracle configurations, per-arm budgets, and the compute
environment.

**Number of runs, and variance reported.** Yes. Every headline comparison is paired within a cell, and the
paper reports the resampling unit for every interval. Three levels of variation are distinguished (target,
nominal seed, execution at a fixed seed) and §5's headline is re-run bit-reproducibly so no execution level
remains.

**Determinism.** Yes, and this took two attempts. The first diagnosis of run-to-run variation was wrong: the
operators call ViennaRNA's `inverse_fold`, whose stochastic search runs on ViennaRNA's *own* global RNG,
invisible to `random.Random(seed)`. `deterministic=True` replaces wall-clock caps with counted budgets and
seeds that generator per attempt. Independently confirmed: `family_grid_par.py` re-runs a serially-produced
seed under 50-way parallelism and gets identical per-target verdicts.

**Statistical significance reported.** Yes, with a caveat the paper states rather than hides: with five
nominal seeds these are *sensitivity intervals*, not calibrated confidence intervals, and we do not use them
as tests. The most robust statement about the headline effect uses no interval at all (strictly higher in
14/15 executions, 15/15 on the structurally disjoint pool). Where clustering matters we cluster: the positive
control's exact McNemar ($5.1\times10^{-37}$) is **not** quoted, because its 951 units come from 60 targets;
the target-clustered permutation $p{=}5\times10^{-5}$ is.

**Multiple comparisons addressed.** Partly, and stated. No family-wise correction is applied across analyses
answering different questions on overlapping data; the paper labels each as quasi-held-out or exploratory and
says that no exploratory $p$-value is a test. Within the attribution study, six features on one dataset is
multiple comparison and is flagged as such; the guard there is not a correction but the requirement that any
survivor face an intervention.

**Compute reported.** Yes, App. F. One 256-core host, target-level parallelism at 60 workers. The entire
sweep suite is search-only and incurs no model-inference cost except the invention agent (a few dollars).

## Not applicable / not done

**No pre-registration.** Stated in §1: "fixed in advance" is our word, and the choice between bootstrap
nesting variants is a degree of freedom a reader cannot audit. The resolution offered is not the choice but
the deterministic re-run that removes it.

**No wet-lab confirmation.** Every verdict is model-relative. §6.2(ii).

**No human subjects, no personally identifying data, no ethics review required.**
